Where Wikipedia fights

Behind some Wikipedia pages, editors keep undoing each other's changes. This site tracks those edit wars across English Wikipedia since 2005: 207,102 pages, ranked year by year by how heated the back-and-forth got. Look up a page you care about to see when it was fought over, or pick a year to see what everyone was arguing about.

    How the score works

    The score counts mutual reverts, pairs of editors who each undid the other's edits on the same page in the same year. Pairs of heavy editors count for more, and the score grows with the number of editors involved. It measures back-and-forth reverting, not who was right, whether a page is biased, or how many people read it.

    More about the method

    Each mutually reverting pair is weighted by the smaller of the two editors' edit counts on the page. The single heaviest pair is dropped, so that two people feuding alone don't top the list. The remaining total is multiplied by the number of editors involved in mutual reverts. Bots are excluded.

    This is the “M” measure from Sumi, Yasseri, Rung, Kornai and Kertész (2011), which Yasseri and colleagues used in 2013 to rank Wikipedia's most controversial articles.

    • Most pages never see an edit war: depending on the year, only 0.2% to 1.5% of edited pages score above zero. The site lists all page-years with a score above zero. A page missing from a year had no edit war that year.
    • Pages with equal scores share a rank. Low scores are common, so many pages tie further down the list.
    • Pages are followed by their ID, so renames don't split a page's history. Titles shown are the current ones.
    • “Kind” is the page's Wikidata type today, and is missing for some pages.
